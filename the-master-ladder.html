The master ladder · v32 · one error, four rungs

One error, four rungs: an instrument takes what it can count for the thing it claims to measure, and the inversion concentrates at the extremes. What changes at each rung is scale, intimacy and visibility — the world, the industry, science, the skull; a visible surface, glass-walled metrics, code few will read, no surface at all — and one escape hatch closes per rung, until the fourth, where there is nowhere left to stand and Part III can begin. — The headline is scoped and graded argued. The headline states the claim in the book’s own terms and the deck defines the two thumbs for a reader meeting them for the first time. Digital covers the first three instruments; the fourth — the one in your head — is named separately in the same sentence, because the ladder’s payoff is that it is not a technology problem. Reaches, not touches: peace touches a person; what it never does is reach the account. It quantifies over accounts that count activity, not over all instruments everywhere: the eligibility clause is the mechanism — to be counted at all, a unit must still be touching the instrument, so the maximally captured produce maximal signal and the freed produce no record at all, which is not the same as a low score. Prevalence in any named platform remains unmeasured: audit broadly, claim pathology narrowly. — Rung 3 is about disposal. Every instrument that scores anything must do something with the cases it cannot score: absorb them into the negative class, exclude them before the metric is computed, or never generate a scorable event. Observation 1 proves the absorbing case for the whole class of rules: any rule whose bad-outcome label requires eligibility must absorb the units that lose it. The author's own pre-registered instrument absorbed (69.2% of the control class, sign reversed); all five external instruments he pre-registered and audited excluded — a different disposal of the same unscorable cases. The open test is the census named on the rung: a systematic review of disposal rules in published benchmarks — for each, what happens to errored, timed-out, filtered and ineligible units, and whether the headline metric discloses it. It produces a taxonomy and a prevalence estimate, and the answers are in the code and the appendices. Nobody has run it. Adjacent work establishes that the layer misreads itself elsewhere too: Northcutt, Athalye and Mueller (NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks 2021) on label errors in ten standard test sets and rankings that invert on corrected labels; Raji, Bender, Paullada, Denton and Hanna (same venue) on construct validity; Kapoor and Narayanan (Patterns 2023) on leakage across 17 fields and 294 papers. — The glass tags record a second fact: the glass is physical, the ladder is an ordering by inspectability, and the narrator sits in front of the glass while being the least inspectable instrument of the four. — Rung 4 is drawn with one pan because the evidence never arrives to be weighed; its sources are McAdams' narrative identity and Gazzaniga's interpreter, and the device is an argued analogy carried by chapter 7's testimony. — The era row is product milestones, not a history of the capability: ranking is older than the surface, and the layer that decides has had its objective retuned without being replaced. The clearest case is 11 January 2018, when Zuckerberg changed the goal given to Facebook’s product teams from helping people find relevant content to helping them have “meaningful social interactions,” expecting time spent to fall. The retune then produced its own inversion: the algorithm gave outsize weight to posts that drew comments and replies, reaction emoji had been weighted five times a like since 2017, and Meta’s own researchers found the change amplified divisive and angry content, with the angry reaction cut to 1.5 and then to zero by 2020. That evidence comes from the 2021 Haugen documents as read by a consortium of outlets — witnessed, not proven. The handover figures ψ 0.5 and 0.9 are alternative scenario settings from Korinek, Jones et al., not an observed trajectory. — The stakes line — the scale that weighs the future is one you cannot inspect — is a statement of what is at issue, not a finding: whether these same failures run through the instruments that will decide which economy we are living in is the open question, not a result. — Evidence grades are the book's own verb law: argued, witnessed, proven, testified.
